# Campaign structure

## The player promise

*Civilian 66* starts below the surface and expands into a larger adventure. The first goal is to survive and establish a place to return to. Later, the player reaches the surface, meets another survivor, learns to read a wider world, and helps a colony become capable of reaching Earth again.

## The journey

| Phase | Player experience | Change in the player's horizon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Mines | Survival, combat, mining, crafting, trade, and settlement building | From isolated crash survivor to builder of a viable foothold |
| The breakthrough | Reconstruct and use a five-part drill | A hard-earned transition from underground to surface |
| The Surface | Exploration, first contact, stealth, and reconnaissance | Survival now depends on learning a world larger than the landing site |
| The Subvergence | Magic transforms a world that did not begin with it | New pressures and abilities reshape familiar survival systems |
| Colony future | Settlement and civilization grow toward a satellite message | The story moves from personal survival to a future for the colony |

The phases change the player's verbs and the meaning of progress. They remain connected by the settlement, the people the player brings into the colony, and the consequences of exploration.

## Character functions

- **Civilian 66** earns a personal name through survival and what the player establishes.
- **Clarissa, Civilian 143,** survived by intelligence, stealth, and opportunism. Meeting her marks a personal change for the player character.
- **Bruce** helps turn a foothold into infrastructure, trade, and repeatable civic life.

## Design questions

- What new action does each phase teach or emphasize?
- What earlier systems remain useful after the genre layer changes?
- How does a transition feel earned through player action?
- How do character relationships change what the player can do?
- How does the campaign scale from an individual to a civilization?

## Status

This case summarizes the current creative direction described in Game Bible v1.10. The public *Civilian 66* repository contains an overview and selected project context. It does not contain the full Bible or promise that each campaign phase is already playable.
